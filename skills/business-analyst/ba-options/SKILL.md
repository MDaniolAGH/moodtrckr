# Options comparison

The cheapest licence is rarely the cheapest option. Compare what the client will actually live with.

## Principles

- Always include: **keep and simplify**, **change the way of working (no or minimal software
  change)**, **use an existing product or service**, and **adapt the existing app**. Remove an
  option only with a stated reason.
- Criteria come from the decision statement and the requirements, not from what is easy to compare.
- **Total cost of ownership** = one-off cost + running cost + the client's own time and duties
  (support, updates, data handling) over the study period.
- Market information must have a source and a date. Unverified prices are marked as such.

## Steps

1. Take the options from the decision statement; add missing ones.
2. Define 4–6 criteria with the client's constraints as hard limits (budget, monthly cost,
   support capacity) and the rest as weighted criteria.
3. For each option: how it meets each Must requirement, one-off and monthly cost, duties that stay
   with the client, main risks.
4. Eliminate options that break a hard limit; compare the rest.

## Output

| Option | Meets Musts? | One-off | Monthly | Client duties (hours/week) | Main risk | Source (date) |
|--------|--------------|---------|---------|----------------------------|-----------|---------------|

Then 2–3 sentences: which options survive the hard limits and why.

Write the output in the language of the client brief.

## Done when

- At least one non-software option was considered.
- Every cost has a source and date, or is marked as an estimate with its basis.
