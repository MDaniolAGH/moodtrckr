# Decision statement

A client rarely needs "an app". They need to make a decision. Name it before proposing anything.

## Principles

- Describe the **decision**, not the solution. "Build a dashboard" is a solution; "decide how
  the two sites will produce comparable summaries" is a decision.
- Options must include ones that are **not software**: keep as is, change the way of working,
  use an existing product or service.
- Use only facts from the brief or from the client. Do not import assumptions from other projects.

## Steps

1. From the brief, extract: who decides, what is at stake, constraints (budget, time, support
   capacity), and what worries the client.
2. List the realistic options the client is choosing between (at least three, at least one
   non-technical).
3. Write the decision in one sentence:
   *"[Who] must decide whether to [option A], [option B] or [option C], so that [goal],
   within [key constraints]."*
4. Read it back as the client: would they agree this is their decision? Remove any feature
   or technology name that the client did not choose.

## Output

```
Decision: <one sentence>
Options considered: <A> | <B> | <C> ...
Source: <brief section or client statement for each element>
```

Write the output in the language of the client brief.

## Done when

- One sentence, no technology or vendor names unless the client chose them.
- Every element traces back to the brief or the client.
