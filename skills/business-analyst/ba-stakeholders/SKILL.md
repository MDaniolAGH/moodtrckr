# Stakeholders and information flows

Requirements come from people, and conflicts between them are the real requirements.

## Principles

- Include those who are affected but not paying: participants, staff with no time for support,
  future maintainers.
- For data about people, ask for every flow: **who provides it, who can see it, for what purpose,
  who decides**. Purpose matters as much as access.
- Record what each stakeholder needs *and* what they must not get or must not have to do.

## Steps

1. List stakeholders from the brief, then add the missing ones (who uses, who supports, who
   maintains, who reads the results, who is affected).
2. For each: goal, main concern, influence on the decision, and expected involvement after launch.
3. Draw the information flows: data item → from whom → to whom → purpose → when.
4. Mark conflicts (e.g. one party wants more detail, another needs less exposure) and flows that
   nobody has agreed yet.

## Output

| Stakeholder | Goal | Concern | Influence | Involvement after launch |
|-------------|------|---------|-----------|--------------------------|

| Data | From | To | Purpose | Agreed? |
|------|------|----|---------|---------|

Then: conflicts and open flows, each with who must resolve it.

Write the output in the language of the client brief.

## Done when

- Every data flow has a stated purpose, or is marked as unagreed.
- At least one stakeholder who is not the client contact is represented.
