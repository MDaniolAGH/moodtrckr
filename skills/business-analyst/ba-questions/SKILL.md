# Questions that change the recommendation

Client time is scarce. Ask only questions whose answers change what you will propose.

## Principles

- A good question is **open, neutral and specific**. It does not suggest the answer or the
  solution ("Would a dashboard help?" is leading; "How is the summary used today, and by whom?"
  is not).
- If no possible answer would change your recommendation, do not ask it.
- One question, one topic.

## Steps

1. Start from the high-impact assumptions and unknowns (see `ba-assumptions`).
2. Turn each into a question the client can actually answer — about their goals, process,
   people and constraints, not about technology they may not know.
3. For each question write two or three plausible answers and what you would propose in each
   case.
4. Rank by impact on the recommendation. Keep 3–5.

## Output

```
1. <question>
   Why it matters: <assumption or unknown it resolves>
   If <answer A> → <effect on proposal>
   If <answer B> → <effect on proposal>
```

Write the output in the language of the client brief.

## Done when

- Every question has at least two answers leading to different proposals.
- No question presumes a solution, a vendor or a defect in the app.
